What remains open

Predictive scores do not establish that a DNA change causes a disease.

01 / THE STORY

What actually happened

Google DeepMind introduced AlphaGenome in June 2025. The system aims to predict aspects of genome regulation and variant effects.

Google DeepMind presents AlphaGenome as a sequence-based approach to predicting regulatory signals and the effect of genetic variants. The project concerns basic biological understanding.

02 / THE STORY

Why this matters

Understanding DNA requires more than reading a string of letters. Regulatory sequences influence when and how genes are expressed, and variants can change those patterns. Models that predict these effects may help researchers prioritize experiments.

An estimated regulatory effect is different from a clinical interpretation of an individual’s genome. Predictions depend on data quality, biology and the specific task being evaluated.

WHY IT MATTERS

A predicted molecular effect is a scientific lead, not proof of a clinical outcome. Experimental confirmation remains critical.

03 / THE STORY

What to be careful about

Predictions can be wrong and depend on the biological context. They require laboratory validation and should not be treated as individual clinical findings.

04 / THE STORY

What to watch next

Track independent benchmarks and studies showing whether researchers can use these predictions to design more informative experiments.

Particularly valuable evidence would come from laboratories using model predictions to select experiments and independently confirming the biological effects.

IN PRACTICE / WHAT TO WATCH

From model prediction to evidence

A computational model can suggest which DNA changes deserve closer examination. This can help guide the design of experiments when the number of possible variations is extremely large.

Biological context is complex. Predictions must be checked against measurements and evaluated in the relevant cell or organism before stronger conclusions can be drawn.

ONE GOOD QUESTION

A question worth asking.

Can a predicted variant effect be reproduced under controlled experimental conditions?

A useful way to assess the announcement beyond the headline.
